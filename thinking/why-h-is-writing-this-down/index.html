Why H is writing this down

1 September 2026 · Starting out · 4 min read

For a while, this looked like it might become a newsletter. It isn’t one. It’s H’s thinking, written down as it happens, and kept somewhere permanent for as long as it stays useful.

Every organisation H works with arrives with a property or infrastructure question that feels specific to them. It rarely is. Underneath the particular building, the particular lease, the particular funder or facilities manager, the questions repeat themselves with almost boring regularity. That repetition is worth writing down.

The same questions, in different buildings

A lease gets signed because there was no time, or no one obviously responsible, to have it looked over first. A maintenance issue gets deferred for a year, then another, until it stops being a maintenance issue and starts being a crisis with a board paper attached. A compliance letter arrives written for an engineer, not for the person who now has to decide what to do about it. A quote comes in, and nobody in the room has enough independent knowledge to know whether it’s reasonable.

None of this is unique to any one organisation. It’s simply what happens when property and infrastructure decisions are made by people whose actual job is something else entirely — running a service, supporting a community, keeping a mission moving — with a building attached to it that nobody trained them to manage.

Most infrastructure problems don’t arrive labelled as infrastructure problems. They arrive as a leaking roof, an anxious funder, or a lease renewal nobody wants to open.

This section is where those patterns get set down as they’re noticed — not as case studies, not as anyone’s story but H’s own observations, and not dressed up as advice that applies to everyone in every situation. Just what tends to be true, and what tends to help.

Why not a newsletter

A newsletter has a rhythm to keep and a subscriber list to grow. That’s a perfectly reasonable thing for some organisations to run, and H already has a short, occasional email — Notes from H — for whoever wants something brief in their inbox now and then.

This is a different kind of thing. It’s not trying to arrive on a schedule, and it’s not trying to be read once and archived. The intention is closer to a reference shelf than a mailing list: pieces that stay where they are, that you can come back to or send someone a direct link to, and that add up over time into something resembling what H actually thinks about property, infrastructure, regulation, investment and the built environment — and about the community and for-purpose organisations trying to do good work inside all of it.

What’s here so far, and what’s coming

Right now, this is the first piece. More will follow as there’s something worth saying — not on a fixed cadence, and not padded out to hit one. Some will be short observations. Some will go deeper into a single recurring problem. None of it will read like case studies or client work, because H doesn’t publish either of those.

If you’re here because something you’re dealing with sounds like one of the patterns above, that’s a reasonable reason to keep reading — or to just say hello and start there instead.
